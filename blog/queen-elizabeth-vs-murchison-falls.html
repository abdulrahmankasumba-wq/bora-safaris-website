Queen Elizabeth vs Murchison Falls: Where Does Uganda Offer the Better Game Drive?

Two remarkable national parks, two different wildlife experiences, and one beautiful country worth exploring.

There are places in Uganda where you can drive for miles and still find something beautiful to look at. A green landscape stretching into the distance, a herd of elephants moving quietly through the grass, or a group of Uganda kob grazing as the morning sun rises. Sometimes, if you are lucky, you may even find a lion resting beneath a tree.

This is part of what makes Uganda special.

We often speak about our country as the Pearl of Africa, but beyond the beautiful name is a country with extraordinary natural treasures. From its mountains and lakes to its forests and open savannah, Uganda has plenty to offer anyone who loves nature and adventure.

When it comes to wildlife safaris, two national parks often come into the conversation: Queen Elizabeth National Park and Murchison Falls National Park.

Both are among Uganda's most important wildlife destinations. Both offer exciting opportunities to see animals in their natural surroundings. However, they are not the same. Their landscapes are different, their wildlife experiences vary, and even a game drive in one park can feel very different from a drive in the other.

So, if you were planning a safari in Uganda and had to choose between the two, which one would give you the better game-drive experience?

Let's explore.

"From the open plains of Murchison to the beautiful landscapes of Queen Elizabeth... our country has places that deserve to be explored, protected and celebrated."

Queen Elizabeth National Park: A Landscape Full of Life

In western Uganda, between Lake George and Lake Edward, lies Queen Elizabeth National Park. Covering approximately 1,978 square kilometres, the park is one of the country's most diverse wildlife destinations.

What makes Queen Elizabeth special is the variety of landscapes found within it. Open grasslands, savannah woodland, crater lakes, wetlands and forests all form part of its natural environment.

For visitors interested in game drives, the Kasenyi Plains are among the most important areas to explore. The open grasslands provide opportunities to see Uganda kob, buffaloes, elephants, warthogs and other animals. The plains are also known for lions, which hunt the Uganda kob found in the area.

An early morning drive through Kasenyi can be particularly rewarding. The air is cooler, the landscape is beginning to brighten, and animals may still be active after the night. There is always a sense of anticipation because you never quite know what might appear along the road.

Further south is the Ishasha sector, famous for its tree climbing lions. Unlike the image many people have of lions resting on the ground, these lions are sometimes found lying across the branches of large fig trees.

It is one of the park's most unusual attractions and a special sight for anyone fortunate enough to encounter them. However, sightings are not guaranteed. The lions move around, and finding them requires patience and a knowledgeable guide.

Queen Elizabeth also offers something beyond the traditional game drive. The Kazinga Channel, which connects Lake George and Lake Edward, is known for its rich wildlife. A boat cruise gives visitors a chance to see hippos, crocodiles, elephants and numerous waterbirds along the banks.

This combination of game drives and water based wildlife viewing gives Queen Elizabeth a character of its own. It is a park where a visitor can experience different sides of Uganda's natural beauty without leaving the protected area.

Murchison Falls National Park: The Beauty of the Open Savannah

Now, travel north-west and the scenery changes.

Murchison Falls National Park is Uganda's largest national park, covering approximately 3,840 square kilometres. It is a destination where open savannah, riverine woodland and the mighty Nile come together.

The northern section of the park is particularly important for traditional game drives. Its open grasslands, scattered trees and palm dotted landscapes create a beautiful setting for wildlife viewing.

The Buligi game tracks are among the best-known routes for exploring this part of the park. Visitors have opportunities to encounter elephants, buffaloes, Rothschild's giraffes, Uganda kob, Jackson's hartebeest and other wildlife.

One of the special attractions of Murchison is its giraffes. Watching a group of these tall animals move across the open grassland is an experience that gives the park much of its character.

The landscape also offers a different kind of game drive experience. In some areas, the open terrain allows visitors to scan long distances for animals. This can be particularly helpful when searching for large mammals or observing herds moving across the plains.

But Murchison is not only about what happens on land. There is another attraction that makes this park one of Uganda's most remarkable destinations.

Murchison Falls: Where the Nile Shows Its Power

You cannot tell the story of Murchison Falls National Park without talking about the falls themselves. They are not simply another attraction within the park. They are the natural wonder that gives this destination much of its identity.

The journey begins with the Victoria Nile, which flows through the park on its way towards Lake Albert. As the river approaches the falls, an enormous volume of water is forced through a narrow rocky gorge, measuring approximately seven metres across. It then plunges about 43 metres down the rocks below.

The sight is extraordinary. Imagine the mighty Nile, one of the world's most famous rivers, gathering into a narrow opening before rushing through with tremendous force. The sound of the water fills the surrounding area, while mist rises into the air.

From a distance, the falls are impressive. But getting closer allows you to appreciate just how powerful the river really is.

There are different ways to experience this natural wonder. A boat cruise along the Nile towards the base of the falls is one of the most memorable activities in the park. Along the journey, visitors can see hippos resting in the water, Nile crocodiles along the riverbanks and birds gathering near the water. Elephants and other animals may also be spotted along the shores.

As the boat approaches the falls, the landscape becomes increasingly dramatic. The sound of rushing water grows louder, and the spray from the falls becomes more noticeable.

Visitors can also make their way to the top of the falls. From this viewpoint, the experience is completely different. You can watch the river approach the narrow gorge and force its way through the rocks before plunging down below.

Standing there, watching the Nile push through such a small opening, gives you a new appreciation of the power of nature.

For anyone visiting Murchison Falls National Park, the waterfall deserves more than a quick photograph. It is an experience worth taking time to appreciate. The game drives may introduce visitors to the park's wildlife, but the falls offer something different: a chance to witness one of Uganda's most powerful natural spectacles.

The Game Drive Comparison: Which Park Comes Out Ahead?

This is where choosing between the two becomes difficult. Both parks offer excellent opportunities, but their strengths are different.

Wildlife and Large Mammals

Murchison Falls has a particularly strong appeal for visitors interested in giraffes, elephants, buffaloes and other large savannah mammals. Its northern sector provides extensive open landscapes where these animals can be observed.

Queen Elizabeth also offers excellent wildlife viewing, particularly around Kasenyi. Its lions, Uganda kob, elephants and buffaloes make the plains an important game-drive destination.

For someone particularly interested in seeing giraffes, Murchison has the advantage. For someone hoping to combine different wildlife habitats and the possibility of seeing tree-climbing lions, Queen Elizabeth has a distinctive attraction.

Game-Drive Landscapes and Visibility

Murchison's open northern savannah can make it easier to spot animals from a distance. Its broad grasslands create the feeling of a traditional African safari.

Queen Elizabeth offers more variety in its scenery. The landscape changes between open plains, woodland, wetlands and crater areas. This variety makes the experience interesting, although vegetation and terrain can affect visibility in different locations.

The Experience Beyond the Vehicle

Queen Elizabeth has the Kazinga Channel, Ishasha and Kyambura Gorge, where visitors can experience activities beyond game drives.

Murchison has the Nile boat cruise, the spectacular falls and opportunities to explore the wider conservation area, including Budongo Forest.

Both parks therefore offer more than simply driving around in search of animals.

What About the Big Five?

A safari comparison would hardly be complete without mentioning Africa's famous Big Five: the lion, leopard, elephant, buffalo and rhinoceros.

Both Queen Elizabeth and Murchison Falls offer opportunities to see lions, leopards, elephants and buffaloes. However, neither park has a wild rhinoceros population.

Visitors hoping to complete the Big Five experience in Uganda can include Ziwa Rhino Sanctuary in their itinerary, where rhinos are protected and can be tracked on foot with guides.

This is an important detail for anyone planning a safari, particularly international visitors who may expect to find all five animals in one national park.

Uganda's Other Savannah Parks Deserve Attention Too

Although Queen Elizabeth and Murchison Falls dominate much of the conversation, Uganda has other beautiful wildlife destinations worth exploring.

Kidepo Valley National Park, in north-eastern Uganda, offers a remote and dramatic safari experience. Its open plains, mountain scenery and wildlife make it one of the country's most distinctive destinations.

Lake Mburo National Park, although smaller, has its own appeal. Visitors can encounter zebras, impalas, elands, buffaloes and other animals. Its location also makes it an attractive option for people travelling from central Uganda.

These parks remind us that Uganda's wildlife tourism has more than two destinations to offer. Each park contributes something different to the country's natural heritage.

More Than a Holiday Destination

There is another side to this conversation that deserves attention.

Our national parks are not simply beautiful places to visit. They are important habitats for wildlife, sources of employment and part of Uganda's natural heritage. Tourism also creates opportunities for guides, drivers, accommodation providers, local businesses and communities living around protected areas.

According to Uganda's tourism statistics, Murchison Falls received 139,304 visitor entries in 2024, while Queen Elizabeth received 124,645. Together, the two parks accounted for 60.4 per cent of recorded visits to national parks and wildlife reserves that year.

These figures show just how important both destinations are to Uganda's wildlife tourism industry.

But their future depends on more than visitor numbers. Conservation, responsible tourism and good relationships between wildlife authorities and neighbouring communities are essential. Protecting wildlife while recognising the needs of people living near these parks remains an important part of the country's conservation story.

So, Which Park Should You Choose?

If your main interest is open savannah game drives, large mammals and the opportunity to see giraffes, Murchison Falls makes a very strong choice. Its wildlife, extensive game drive areas and the extraordinary experience of seeing the Nile squeeze through the narrow gorge make it a destination with much to offer.

If you want a variety of landscapes, game drives, the possibility of seeing tree climbing lions and a memorable boat cruise along the Kazinga Channel, Queen Elizabeth deserves serious consideration.

Neither park can promise particular animal sightings. Wildlife moves freely, and every game drive is different. The time of day, season, route and experience of your guide can all influence what you see.

Perhaps that is part of the beauty of a safari. You set out with expectations, but nature decides what the day will bring.

And for those of us who call Uganda home, these parks are a reminder of something worth appreciating. Sometimes, we become so familiar with our surroundings that we forget how extraordinary they are.

From the open plains of Murchison to the beautiful landscapes of Queen Elizabeth, from the mighty Nile at Murchison Falls to the wildlife-rich Kazinga Channel, our country has places that deserve to be explored, protected and celebrated.

The real winner may not be one park over the other. It may simply be the opportunity to experience both and discover, for yourself, just how much beauty Uganda holds.
