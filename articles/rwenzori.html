Rwenzori Mountains - The Legendary Mountains of the Moon

November 2024 8 min read Western Uganda

Rising majestically along the Uganda–DR Congo border, Rwenzori Mountains National Park is home to one of Africa's most breathtaking and mysterious mountain ranges. Known as the Mountains of the Moon, this 120 km-long range offers snow-capped peaks, lush montane forests, bamboo woodlands, and high-altitude moorlands. The Rwenzoris were gazetted as a national park in 1991, designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1994, and recognised as a Ramsar Wetland in 2008.

First documented by Ptolemy in AD 150 as the source of the Nile, and officially mapped by explorer Henry Stanley in 1888 — the Rwenzoris have inspired adventurers for thousands of years.

Margherita Peak at 5,109 metres — the third-highest point in Africa and the crown of the legendary Mountains of the Moon.

The Roof of Uganda: Margherita Peak

The highest point in the Rwenzoris is Margherita Peak on Mount Stanley, standing at 5,109 metres — Africa's third-highest peak. Unlike volcanic giants like Kilimanjaro, the Rwenzoris were formed by tectonic activity, with massive rock blocks thrust upward from the Western Rift Valley. In 2010, Ms. Beryl Park became the oldest recorded climber to reach Margherita Peak at the age of 78.

Key Facts

  • UNESCO World Heritage Site (1994)
  • Ramsar Wetland (2008)
  • Gazetted: 1991
  • Margherita Peak: 5,109 m
  • Africa's 3rd highest peak
  • 70+ mammal species
  • 217 bird species
  • 19 Albertine Rift endemics

Flora, Fauna and Trekking Adventures

The park shelters over 70 mammal species, 217 bird species with 19 Albertine Rift endemics, and some of the world's rarest plant species. Trekking options range from the 9–12 day technical summit ascent of Margherita Peak to mid-level alpine treks and guided cultural walks through Bakonzo villages featuring nature trails, homestead visits, traditional music, and authentic local cuisine.

Why Visit?

  • Summit Africa's third-highest peak — a mountaineering dream
  • Explore a UNESCO World Heritage Site of global significance
  • Discover unique endemic flora and Albertine Rift bird species
  • Trek routes available for all fitness levels
  • Experience rich Bakonzo cultural traditions
  • Witness glaciers, alpine valleys, and giant lobelia forests
