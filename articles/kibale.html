Kibale Forest – The Primate Capital of the World

February 2025 7 min read Western Uganda

Step into the lush tropical rainforest of Kibale National Park, widely known as the Primate Capital of the World. This extraordinary forest hosts the famous Kanyanchu Primate Walk — an unforgettable adventure giving visitors access to 13 different primate species, many regularly seen swinging through the canopy.

With over 1,500 wild chimpanzees and a 90%+ sighting success rate, Kibale offers one of the most reliable and thrilling primate encounters on Earth.

Chimpanzee Tracking

Chimpanzee tracking walks run daily at 8:00 AM, 11:00 AM, and 2:00 PM. Treks last two to five hours; once the group is found, visitors spend one full hour quietly observing their fascinating behaviours — grooming, feeding, and playful interactions. Each group is limited to six participants and visitors must be at least 16 years old.

A wild chimpanzee in Kibale Forest — home to over 1,500 chimps and 13 primate species.

Key Facts About Kibale

  • 13 primate species
  • 1,500+ wild chimpanzees
  • 90%+ sighting success
  • 370+ bird species
  • 120+ mammal species
  • Gazetted: 1993
  • Size: 795 km²
  • Elevation: 1,100–1,590 m

One of Kibale's 13 primate species perched in the lush forest canopy.

Wildlife and Birdlife

Beyond primates, the forest supports Olive Baboon, African Forest Elephant, Bush Pig, and Duiker. For birdwatchers, Kibale is exceptional — 370 recorded species including the rare Nahan's Francolin, Cassin's Spinetail, Blue-headed Bee-eater, and Lowland Masked Apalis.

Why Visit?

  • Track wild chimpanzees with a 90%+ sighting rate
  • Encounter 13 primate species in one forest
  • Discover 370+ bird species including rare Albertine Rift endemics
  • Explore one of Africa's richest tropical ecosystems
  • Enjoy an immersive jungle experience with expert ranger guides
