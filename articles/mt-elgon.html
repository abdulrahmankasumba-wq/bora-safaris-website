Mount Elgon – The Ancient Giant of East Africa

October 2024 7 min read Eastern Uganda / Kenya Border

Rising majestically along the Uganda–Kenya border, Mount Elgon National Park is home to one of East Africa's most fascinating and ancient mountains. Mount Elgon is an extinct volcano that first erupted over 24 million years ago, making it one of the oldest volcanic formations in the region. Millions of years ago, Mount Elgon was believed to be Africa's tallest mountain, even higher than Kilimanjaro. Natural erosion gradually reduced its height to 4,321 metres — today the fourth-highest peak in East Africa.

Unlike many mountains where reaching the summit is the main objective, trekking Mount Elgon is about discovering its extraordinary landscapes along the entire journey — waterfalls, caves, calderas, and cultural encounters.

The spectacular landscapes of Mount Elgon National Park — home to the world's largest volcanic caldera.

A Mountain of Culture and the World's Largest Volcanic Base

The slopes of Mount Elgon are home to the Bagisu (BaMasaba), Sabiny, and Ndorobo communities. To the Bagisu, the mountain represents their legendary ancestral father Masaba. One of the region's most famous cultural traditions is the Imbalu Circumcision Ceremony — a vibrant public initiation ritual held every even-numbered year, drawing visitors from across Uganda and beyond. Mount Elgon also holds the record for the world's largest volcanic base at 4,000 km², spanning nearly 80 km in diameter.

Key Facts

  • 4th highest in East Africa: 4,321 m
  • World's largest volcanic base: 4,000 km²
  • Formed 24 million years ago
  • UNESCO Biosphere Reserve
  • 300+ bird species
  • Caldera: 40 km² — one of world's largest
  • Sipi Falls: 3-tier waterfall
  • Kitum Cave: elephant salt mine

The stunning Sipi Falls — a series of three waterfalls cascading down the western slopes of Mount Elgon.

Wildlife, Birdlife and Trekking Highlights

The park supports over 300 bird species including the Lammergeier, Tacazze Sunbird, and Jackson's Francolin. Mammals include Blue Monkey, Black-and-white Colobus, Bushbuck, Duiker, and African Forest Elephant. Key trekking attractions include Wagagai Peak (4,321m), the vast 40 km² volcanic caldera, Sipi Falls, natural hot springs, and Kitum Cave — where elephants dig for mineral-rich salt. The mountain holds UNESCO Man and Biosphere Reserve status.

Why Visit?

  • Explore spectacular landscapes from rainforest to alpine moorlands
  • Experience the rich Bagisu, Sabiny, and Ndorobo cultural heritage
  • Discover rare birds, primates, and forest elephants
  • Enjoy peaceful trekking on uncrowded mountain trails
  • Visit a UNESCO-recognised Biosphere Reserve
  • Experience waterfalls, caves, and one of the world's largest calderas
