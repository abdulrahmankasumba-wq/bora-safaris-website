Forest Treasure

Gishwati-Mukura National Park
Discover Rwanda's Quieter Wild Side

August 2026 7 min read Lake Kivu Region, W Rwanda

Not every great safari experience needs crowds, roaring lions or famous landmarks.

Sometimes the most memorable journeys take you somewhere quieter.

Somewhere you can walk beneath ancient trees, listen to birds calling through the forest, search for chimpanzees in the canopy and meet communities whose lives are closely connected to the surrounding landscape.

This is Gishwati-Mukura National Park, Rwanda's fourth and newest national park.

Located in western Rwanda above the beautiful Lake Kivu region, the park protects two separate forest blocks: Gishwati Forest and Mukura Forest. Together, they form an important part of Rwanda's Albertine Rift landscape.

"Come to Gishwati-Mukura to slow down, explore deeper, and discover the quieter side of Rwanda's wild beauty."

A Forest Full of Life

Gishwati-Mukura may not have the enormous open plains of Akagera or the international fame of Volcanoes, but its forests contain an impressive variety of wildlife.

Gishwati is home to a group of approximately 20 chimpanzees, along with golden monkeys, L'Hoest's monkeys and blue monkeys.

The forest also provides habitat for numerous bird species, butterflies and smaller mammals. For nature lovers, the experience is less about ticking animals off a list and more about becoming immersed in the forest ecosystem. Every trail offers something different.

Search for Chimpanzees

Chimpanzee tracking is one of the most exciting experiences available in Gishwati.

Moving through the forest, your guide helps you look and listen for signs of the chimpanzees. The anticipation grows as you move deeper into the trees.

Then you may hear them calling somewhere above you. The encounter can feel particularly intimate because Gishwati offers a quieter and less heavily visited forest experience than some of Rwanda's more famous destinations.

Key Facts About Gishwati-Mukura

  • Rwanda's 4th & newest national park (Established 2015)
  • Two forest blocks: Gishwati & Mukura (~34 km²)
  • Home to ~20 habituated Chimpanzees
  • 232 bird species in Gishwati + 163 in Mukura
  • Albertine Rift endemic species & primates
  • Rich community tours (Tea plantations, beekeeping, farm stays)
  • Location: Western Rwanda near Lake Kivu

Explore the Forest on Foot

One of the best ways to experience Gishwati-Mukura is simply to walk.

Guided nature walks take travelers into the forest where they can discover plants, insects, birds and primates while learning about the ecosystem. The slower pace allows you to appreciate details that are easy to miss from a safari vehicle.

Sunlight filtering through the canopy. The sound of leaves moving overhead. A colorful butterfly crossing the trail. A monkey watching from a distant branch. It is the kind of experience that encourages you to slow down and pay attention.

A Haven for Birdwatchers

Bird lovers will find plenty to explore.

Gishwati has recorded around 232 bird species, while Mukura has recorded around 163 species.

The forests are especially valuable for species associated with the Albertine Rift, making the park an appealing destination for visitors interested in Rwanda's unique birdlife.

Connect Wildlife With Local Communities

One of the most interesting aspects of Gishwati-Mukura is the opportunity to combine nature with community experiences.

Visitors can explore tea plantations, experience farm stays, learn about beekeeping, participate in handicraft activities and experience cultural performances. Traditional-healer experiences can also provide insight into local knowledge and relationships with the natural environment.

These activities create a safari experience that extends beyond wildlife. Instead of simply observing Rwanda's landscapes, travelers can interact with the people who live alongside them.

A Conservation Story

Gishwati-Mukura is also a powerful example of environmental restoration.

The national park was formally established in 2015, with conservation and restoration efforts responding to challenges including deforestation, illegal mining, soil erosion and habitat loss. The forests cover approximately 34 square kilometres, excluding the buffer zone.

Protecting these remaining forests is important for wildlife, water systems, biodiversity and the communities surrounding them. By visiting the park responsibly, travelers can become part of the wider effort to preserve this unique ecosystem.

Combine the Forest With Lake Kivu

The park's location in western Rwanda makes it particularly attractive for travelers who want to combine wildlife with the spectacular scenery of the Lake Kivu region.

A journey through Gishwati-Mukura can therefore become more than a forest safari. You can combine nature walks and primate tracking with tea plantations, community experiences and the beautiful landscapes around Lake Kivu.

Why Visit Gishwati-Mukura National Park?

  • Discover Rwanda's newest national park and a inspiring conservation success story
  • Trek chimpanzees and golden monkeys in a peaceful, uncrowded forest setting
  • Explore over 395 combined bird species across Gishwati and Mukura forests
  • Engage in community tours, tea plantation visits, and traditional cultural experiences
  • Seamlessly combine forest trekking with relaxation on the shores of Lake Kivu

Gishwati-Mukura is perfect for travelers searching for a quieter, more intimate and community-oriented nature experience. It may be Rwanda's newest national park, but its forests represent something much older: a living ecosystem that has survived generations of environmental change.
