Queen Elizabeth National Park
Uganda's Crown Jewel of Biodiversity

March 2025 8 min read Western Uganda

Queen Elizabeth National Park is Uganda's most popular and biodiverse safari destination, home to over 95 mammal species and more than 600 bird species. Originally established in 1952, it straddles the equator against the backdrop of the Rwenzori Mountains.

The Famous Tree-Climbing Lions of Ishasha

In the Ishasha Sector, lions spend hours lounging in large fig trees — a rare behaviour seen almost nowhere else in the world. These lions can often be seen resting high in the branches while watching Uganda Kob on the savanna below.

"Watching a lion gaze down at you from the branches of a fig tree is one of the most extraordinary wildlife moments in Africa."

The Kazinga Channel

The 40-km Kazinga Channel connects Lake Edward and Lake George. Boat safaris here reveal hundreds of hippos, massive Nile crocodiles, herds of elephants and buffaloes, and colourful waterbirds including pink-backed pelicans and saddle-billed storks.

Key Facts

  • Established 1952, renamed 1954
  • 95+ mammal species
  • 600+ bird species
  • 20 carnivore species
  • Straddles the equator
  • Size: 1,978 km²
  • Kazinga Channel: 40 km
  • Elevation: 910–1,350 m

African buffalo herds are among the most commonly sighted Big Five animals in Queen Elizabeth National Park.

Wildlife Beyond the Lions

The park hosts Elephants, Buffaloes, Hippos, Waterbuck, Leopards, Spotted Hyenas, Chimpanzees, Black-and-white Colobus, Vervet Monkeys, and Olive Baboons. For birders, the African Fish Eagle, Shoebill, and Papyrus Gonolek are among the highlights.

Cultural Experiences

Visitors can enjoy cultural tours in Bakonzo, Basongora, and Banyabindi communities — traditional storytelling, music, dance, and insights into lifestyles that have coexisted with wildlife for generations.

Why Visit Queen Elizabeth National Park?

  • See the world-famous tree-climbing lions of Ishasha
  • Cruise the Kazinga Channel for wildlife encounters
  • Spot 600+ bird species — Uganda's top birding destination
  • Experience vibrant local community culture
  • Explore one of Uganda's oldest national parks
