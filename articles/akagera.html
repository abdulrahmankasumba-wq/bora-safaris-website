Akagera National Park
Rwanda’s Big Five Safari Adventure

August 2026 7 min read Eastern Rwanda

Imagine waking before sunrise, stepping out into the cool African morning, and watching the horizon slowly turn golden. In the distance, a herd of zebra moves across the grassland while giraffes stretch their long necks above the woodland. Somewhere nearby, a lion may be preparing for its morning hunt.

This is Akagera National Park, Rwanda's great savannah wilderness and the country's premier destination for a traditional African wildlife safari.

Located in eastern Rwanda along the border with Tanzania, Akagera is a striking contrast to the misty volcanic mountains and dense rainforests found elsewhere in the country. Here, rolling savannah plains meet woodland, wetlands and a chain of beautiful lakes, creating an extraordinary landscape for wildlife and adventure.

"Akagera is where Rwanda's wild heart beats across the savannah — offering the classic African Big Five safari alongside shimmering lakes and a triumphant conservation story."

A Big Five Safari in the Heart of Rwanda

Akagera is Rwanda's principal savannah safari destination and is particularly exciting because of its successful wildlife restoration efforts.

The park is home to Africa's famous Big Five: lion, leopard, elephant, buffalo and rhinoceros. Although wildlife sightings can never be guaranteed, the possibility of encountering these iconic animals is one of the major reasons travelers visit Akagera.

You might find a pride of lions resting beneath a tree after a night of hunting, watch elephants moving through the woodland, or encounter enormous buffalo herds grazing across the plains.

The park is also home to giraffes, zebras, antelopes, hippos, Nile crocodiles, olive baboons and vervet monkeys.

For photographers, the landscape offers countless opportunities. A giraffe silhouetted against the sunrise, zebra crossing an open plain, or a hippo emerging from the waters of Lake Ihema can become the kind of safari memory that stays with you long after you return home.

Key Facts About Akagera National Park

  • Home to the Big Five (Lion, Leopard, Elephant, Buffalo, Rhino)
  • 490+ recorded bird species
  • Lake Ihema boat safaris
  • Reintroduced Lions & Eastern Black Rhinos
  • Area: 1,122 km²
  • Location: Eastern Rwanda
  • Ecosystem: Savannah, wetlands, lakes & woodland

Discover Akagera on a Game Drive

The classic way to experience Akagera is through a game drive.

As your safari vehicle moves quietly through the park, every bend in the road brings the possibility of something unexpected. Experienced guides read animal tracks, study movements and know where different species are commonly found.

Early-morning drives are particularly rewarding because many animals are active before the heat of the day arrives. As the sun rises over the savannah, the park gradually comes alive.

For an entirely different atmosphere, consider a night drive. Under the African night sky, the experience becomes more mysterious. Nocturnal animals emerge, predators become active and the familiar savannah takes on a completely different character.

Cruise Across Lake Ihema

Akagera isn't only about land.

One of the park's most memorable experiences is a boat safari on Lake Ihema.

Gliding across the lake provides an opportunity to observe wildlife from an entirely different perspective. Hippos gather in the water, crocodiles lie motionless along the shoreline, and numerous waterbirds move between the wetlands.

The peaceful rhythm of the boat contrasts beautifully with the excitement of a game drive. For many visitors, the Lake Ihema boat trip becomes one of the most relaxing experiences of their Rwanda safari.

A Paradise for Birdwatchers

Akagera is also an exceptional destination for bird lovers, with around 490 bird species recorded.

The park's combination of lakes, wetlands, woodland and open savannah creates habitats for an impressive variety of birds. Even travelers who aren't dedicated birdwatchers will find themselves stopping to admire the colorful species that appear along the safari route.

A Conservation Success Story

Akagera is more than a place to see wildlife. It is also an important example of conservation and ecological restoration.

Lions were reintroduced to the park, while eastern black rhinoceroses have also been reintroduced as part of efforts to restore wildlife populations. Strong anti-poaching measures have included canine units, helicopter patrols and dedicated rhino trackers.

A visit to Akagera therefore contributes to a conservation story that demonstrates what can happen when wildlife protection, tourism and local communities work together.

Why Visit Akagera National Park?

  • Experience Rwanda's only Big Five savannah safari destination
  • Enjoy scenic boat cruises on Lake Ihema among hippos and crocodiles
  • Spot nearly 490 bird species across wetlands and open savannahs
  • Witness one of Africa's most successful wildlife conservation & reintroduction stories
  • Take thrilling day and nocturnal night game drives

If your idea of an African safari includes lions, elephants, giraffes, rhinos, zebras, hippos and wide-open savannah, Akagera should be high on your Rwanda itinerary. It offers the classic African safari experience while adding something distinctly Rwandan: beautiful lakes, peaceful landscapes and a remarkable conservation story.
